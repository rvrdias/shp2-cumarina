SHP2 × coumarin · modeling

Supplementary material · computational modeling

Where coumarin fits on SHP2

The experiments in this work test whether, and how strongly, coumarin interacts with SHP2. The modeling answers a different question: assuming binding occurs, where — and it points, reproducibly, to the allosteric tunnel, the same cavity used by known SHP2 inhibitors.

A companion to the poster, covering the methods and results of the computational part, carried out in collaboration with the experimental group.

The protein

A switch that folds onto itself

SHP2 is a phosphatase that turns cell-growth signaling on and off. It has two SH2 domains in tandem — N-SH2 and C-SH2 — followed by the catalytic PTP domain. In its basal, closed form the N-SH2 folds over the PTP and caps the catalytic site; when the protein opens, that site becomes accessible. The disease-associated E76K mutation hinders closing and keeps the protein in the active form for longer.

How it was done

Blind docking, repeated hundreds of times

  1. Co-folding with Boltz-2

    The model received the SHP2 sequence and the coumarin structure and assembled the two together in space, with no information about where coumarin should bind (blind docking).

  2. No fixed reference structure

    We started from the normal protein (WT) and did not force the active (E76K) form. Leaving the model free avoids inducing a conformation; how coumarin binds the open form is future work.

  3. Broad sampling

    The calculation was repeated across 533 independent predictions, each from a different random start — the computational equivalent of repeating the experiment many times.

  4. Analysis

    For each structure we measured where coumarin landed, the conformational state (closed or open), and the residues neighboring the ligand.

What we found

A site that recurs

Coumarin converges on the allosteric tunnel

In about 98% of the 533 predictions, coumarin ended up in the same narrow cavity, where the domains meet in the closed form — the same one that accommodates described SHP2 inhibitors (such as SHP099).

Tryptophans sit beside the pocket

The two tryptophans closest to coumarin in the model — W112 and W248, about 8–9 Å away — offer a structural rationale consistent with the fluorescence quenching seen in the experiments.

WT and E76K behave the same

The predicted binding was essentially identical in both forms of the protein — consistent with residue 76 lying far from the pocket (≈17 Å), too far to touch coumarin.

The conformation was not imposed

Left free, the vast majority of predictions stayed in the closed form, and coumarin occupied the same tunnel regardless of conformation.

Reading the 533 predictions

An honest reading

What the model shows — and what it doesn't

shows

Where a possible interaction would occur: the allosteric tunnel, reproducibly across hundreds of independent predictions.

doesn't

Whether coumarin binds, or how strong the interaction is. The tunnel is a strong attractor in blind docking — molecules that are not SHP2 binders also land in it — so the convergence points to the likely site, not to binding itself.

If binding occurs, the predicted site is the allosteric tunnel. Whether it binds, and how strongly, is for the experiments to settle.
